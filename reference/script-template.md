# Project Speare — Lightweight Script Template
**Updated: 18 October 2026.** Use [the canonical playbook](../strategy/project-speare-playbook.md) and [active skill](../.claude/skills/project-speare/SKILL.md). This is an aid to planning, **not a mandated structure**.

**Working title / thumbnail promise:** What can a new viewer expect to learn?
**Game / version / audience:** What relevant mechanics can we assume?
**Central question:** What needs investigating?
**Provisional answer:** What would the evidence currently support, and what could change it?
**Research ledger:** Link primary sources, dates, competing interpretations, specific mechanics and factual unknowns.
**Narrative engine:** Causal critique / systems mystery / gameplay behavior / chronological obstacles / overlooked history / other.
**Target length:** As long as the necessary story takes; do not pad to a number.

## Opening
Start with a recognizable event, issue or interesting question. Establish the exact object and start satisfying the title promise. Avoid generic setup, manufactured drama or exaggerated stakes.

## Major sections (repeat only as needed)
**Question:** What does the viewer want to know *now*?
**New evidence:** One or more facts, examples, actual game decisions or strong counterexamples.
**Reasoning:** What do they establish? Where does this complicate the explanation?
**Changed understanding:** What can the viewer now conclude that they could not earlier?
**Necessary next development:** Why does the next section follow?

The five lines above are for **planning**, not voiceover.

## Ending
Land the strongest answer the verified material supports. A concrete consequence, historical outcome, unusual detail or understated personal response is often enough. No compulsory recap or universal life lesson.

## Recording voice & editing
Natural, informed gamer-to-gamer talk. Use XP, gear, BiS, DPS, ticks, raids and other game vocabulary when relevant. **Avoid singular “player” for an individual; plural “players” is fine.** Write varied, speakable sentences. Do not overexplain game basics, add defensive disclaimers or endlessly circle the conclusion. Visually, use obtainable footage, game assets, stock, simple generated stills or basic motion; add specific [VISUAL:] cues only if helpful for the requested draft.

Final check: Does every section *change* what we know? Does the argument get deeper as it goes? Is each major claim backed by evidence? Do the transitions arise from actual logic? Can redundant sentences be removed? Is the language genuinely comfortable to say?
