This file and the paired [active skill](../.claude/skills/project-speare/SKILL.md) replace contradictory instructions in old style studies, `CLAUDE.md`, archived creator guides, templates and past draft prompts. ## 1. Objective

Produce deeply researched, unusually well-structured gaming videos that feel natural to listen to. Each section should make the viewer understand something *new* and create a reason to hear what follows. The argument should get **more precise and consequential**, not merely longer, louder or more repetitive.

The core craft reference is **Folding Ideas (Dan Olson)**, informed by a ten-work study of his sentence construction, paragraph flow, evidence ordering, argument escalation, and endings. Transfer the techniques, **never reproduce distinctive wording, signature jokes, catchphrases, or his persona**. The final voice remains the user's own, calibrated from their manually supplied and approved edited scripts.

Other complementary influences:
- **Defunctland:** lead with a concrete question; explain systems through consequences; stronger new evidence may cause earlier facts to be interpreted differently.
- **NakeyJakey:** recognizable in-game experiences, conversational wording, concrete actions, useful analogy, humor without academic stiffness.
- **Summoning Salt:** make goals, constraints and obstacles understandable *before* recording progress, near-misses and breakthroughs.
- **Jon Bois:** select historical and human details that gain meaning as the story develops; allow an understated ending once the material earns it.

Choose the narrative engine that fits the subject. An analysis of a design decision need not sound like a speedrun history; a direct news story need not have a twist.

## 2. Folding Ideas — primary sentence and paragraph standard

1. **Ordinary language, sophisticated thought.** Prefer intelligible main clauses, specific nouns and precise verbs. Complex reasoning does not require ornate wording or formal academic phrasing.
2. **Write connected thoughts, not isolated punchlines.** Let a sentence follow naturally from the claim before it: identify a cause, test an implication, add a necessary condition, surface a problem, or advance an interpretation. Do not fill gaps with "here's where it gets interesting."
3. **Use connectors for logic.** "So," "well," "though," "because," "but," "anyway," and "for example" are available spoken tools. Use one when it genuinely signals consequence, qualification, reversal or return; don't inject filler to manufacture voice.
4. **Vary sentences by function.** A longer sentence can carry a causal chain, a comparison, or a qualification that belongs in the same thought. A short sentence can reveal a problem, resolve an argument or reverse an expectation. Constant fragments and identical rhythms cheapen both.
5. **Make the claim precise.** Narrow or qualify only when truth requires it. Avoid defensively narrating your own caution or repeatedly saying what the video is not claiming.
6. **Move between concrete and abstract.** Start with observable mechanics, choices, costs, XP, gear, incentives and outcomes; then explain the underlying system. It is also valid to state a precise analytical rule first and immediately test it against an in-game example.
7. **Give evidence distinct jobs.** One example establishes the issue; a later example tests an excuse, identifies its cause, introduces a counterexample or reveals a structural consequence. Five interchangeable examples are usually weaker than two that change the diagnosis.
8. **Escalate explanatory power.** Investigate whether a problem is a temporary implementation/balance issue, an incentive problem, a design tradeoff, or inherent in the underlying concept. Don't claim structural failure until the evidence supports it.
9. **Place conclusions after their support.** A concise judgment or memorable metaphor lands when the audience understands the mechanism already. Do not announce the grand conclusion and spend three paragraphs paraphrasing it.
10. **Return to earlier concepts only when they mean more now.** A callback is effective when the new evidence changes its interpretation. Avoid circular restatement for atmosphere.
11. **Allow texture and understatement.** Light irony, specificity, a knowing gaming aside, or an unusual but precise metaphor can convey personality. Avoid obligatory jokes, grand declarations, and constant formal rhetorical contrast.
12. **Write a defensible ending.** Answer the question promised by the opening at the precision warranted by sources; if an earned scene, observation or finding expresses it, stop there.

**Critical:** Sentence-level naturalness comes from following the reasoning. Do not pursue "Dan Olson-like" surface phrasing, cadence or stock openings independently of content.

## 3. The narrative engine

Work toward **one cumulative argument, investigation or historical story**.

Useful paths (examples, not rigid templates):
- **Design inquiry:** observed mechanic → actual incentive → plausible behavior → measurable consequence → deeper design tradeoff.
- **Investigative reversal:** plausible first explanation → evidence supporting it → unresolved anomaly → better evidence → revised explanation.
- **Historical breakthrough:** objective → constraints → failed attempts and partial solutions → new discovery → consequence.
- **Lore/history puzzle:** specific oddity → relevant historical facts → competing readings → best supported interpretation.
- **Positive analysis:** familiar success → explain how the component works → establish the mechanism that makes it valuable → consequences or limits.

Narrative suspense must arise from a real uncertainty, obstacle or logical implication. No manufactured cliffhangers. A strong direct news story does not need a contrarian spin.

**Mandatory internal check for each major section:**
1. What question or problem is motivating the viewer right now?
2. What new, specific evidence or event is introduced?
3. What precisely does it establish (including limitations and alternatives)?
4. How is the viewer's understanding meaningfully different afterward?
5. Why is the next development needed?

These are five *editorial questions*, not five mandatory sentences or paragraphs. Remove, merge or rethink sections that cannot pass. The middle of the video should contain actual work: analysis, causal explanation, decisive discoveries—not just setup before a final moral.

Transitions should follow the logic or chronology of what has just been established. The best transition often appears to be simply the next necessary sentence.

## 4. End-to-end workflow

### A. Ideation and packaging
Seek a meaningful question, actual gamer audience, evidence-backed original contribution, a credible reason to care and a truthful title/thumbnail promise. Scan fresh community interest and news when timeliness matters, but do not manufacture a unique spin if the direct story is already strong. Reject subjects that cannot carry the proposed length without padding. Avoid applying a rigid video structure before discovering the evidence.

### B. Research
Prefer primary sources, dated developer statements, patch notes, game data, official posts, contemporaneous material and reliable reporting. Record game/version, era and status (live, beta/PTR, announced or speculation). For every consequential point, retain the precise claim, source, factual scope and counterevidence. Separate **documented fact, observed behavior, community report, inference, hypothesis and opinion**.

Research mechanisms: **rules → incentives → actual choices/behavior → consequences**. Incentives alone cannot prove that everyone acted a certain way. Investigate plausible alternative explanations and counterexamples. Hunt for facts that might *change* the thesis, not just confirm it. Lore claims require explicit differentiation between canon, later retcon and interpretation.

### C. Structure
Identify the strongest 3–5 actual developments (not simply topics) and decide which order makes the explanation build. Establish the normal state before a documented reversal and the obstacle before its breakthrough. Reserve longer sections for hard, consequential reasoning, not generic context. Create a concise argument map showing what each section proves and how it changes the next. Choose chapter count and runtime from the material.

### D. Draft the script
Open with the real question, contradiction, situation or finding promised by the title. Speak as an informed gamer to knowledgeable WoW/OSRS viewers. State concrete gameplay details freely without classroom definitions. Build explanation through connected paragraphs with naturally varying sentences. Write useful transitions, selective humor and personal reactions supported by the user's perspective. When a game mechanic changes incentives, show the specific action or tradeoff that would follow, and be explicit if behavior is hypothetical rather than observed. Avoid decorative hypothetical micro-scenarios.

### E. Editorial revision
Review in this order: **accuracy and claim scope → evidence/causal support → progression and missing reasoning → audience assumptions → redundancy → sentence flow and read-aloud rhythm → payoff and ending → solo-creator visual practicality**.

Delete content that adds no new evidence, understanding, orientation, useful humor or meaningful texture. Retain brief spoken reinforcement when it helps audio comprehension. An important counterexample should be allowed to narrow or change the conclusion. If the intended payoff is unsupported, change it.

### F. Learning after publication
When a recording and analytics are provided, evaluate the final spoken wording alongside retention, packaging, visuals, traffic mix and audience familiarity. Avoid treating a retention dip or spike as definitive proof of a writing cause. User edits to approved scripts remain the best evidence for voice calibration.

## 5. Non-negotiable spoken voice

- **Game-native vocabulary is encouraged:** XP, gear, BiS, DPS, raids, bosses, ticks, GCD, aggro, threat, loot, specs, builds, drop rates, buffs, nerfs, skilling, ironman, grind, etc., used accurately.
- **Avoid singular "player" for one individual** ("a player", "the player", "each player"). Prefer **you**, **someone**, **a raider**, **a mage**, **a character**, **an ironman**, another fitting role, or restructure the sentence. **Plural "players" is fine.**
- Do not teach common WoW or OSRS mechanics to people who already play the game. Explain unusual historical facts, obscure connections and unfamiliar consequences only as needed.
- Sound conversational, confident and down to earth; use familiar English rather than polished-but-empty writing. Filler ("so", "well", "I mean", "anyway") is welcome when it fits, not as a quota.
- Avoid narration word **"distinction"**; greatly reduce repeated **"not X, but Y"** framing; avoid superficial claims of importance, performative caveats ("I don't want to pretend…", "this doesn't mean…"), rhetorical formula loops, triads, dramatic fragments and decorative time references.
- Avoid assigning imagined thoughts or actions to the user; do not invent personal gaming experiences, first-person anecdotes, developer motives, numbers, community consensus or fabricated quotes.
- Do not overuse inanimate subjects performing artificial actions. Ordinary metaphor and active voice are fine when specific and meaningful.
- Keep natural dry humor, occasional aside, direct address, and idiosyncrasy from **user-approved edited scripts**. Do not convert a personal, knowledgeable gaming channel into a solemn academic documentary.

## 6. Final quality gate

Before delivery, privately ask:
- Does the opening create a real reason to watch, consistent with the title?
- Which passage establishes each core claim? Which later evidence **deepens, corrects or complicates** it?
- Is the causal link proven, merely plausible or hypothetical? Have I labeled it honestly?
- Can I identify what new understanding each section leaves behind?
- Could the transition be the next logical sentence instead of a formulaic announcement?
- Is a technical explanation necessary for this audience, or am I repeating basics?
- Is any paragraph just another version of a conclusion already delivered?
- Can I read the script naturally with varied rhythm and game-native vocabulary?
- Do the strongest short sentences arrive **after** the reasoning they depend on?
- Have I fulfilled the opening promise and stopped without a second conclusion?
