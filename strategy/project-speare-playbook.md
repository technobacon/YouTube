# Project Speare — Canonical Editorial Playbook
**Version 2.0 · 18 October 2026 · HIGHEST PRIORITY**

This is the governing editorial standard for gaming-video ideation, research, structure, writing, revision and post-publication analysis. It supersedes conflicting style rules in `CLAUDE.md`, `exurb1a.md`, old scripts, templates and historical research files. Preserve earlier source-backed research as evidence and the user's approved edits as voice calibration. Do not imitate any individual YouTuber's signature language.

## One continuous analytical story
The viewer should understand something meaningfully new at every section. Build from an actual puzzle through evidence, causes, choices, behavior, obstacles, consequences and earned findings. Progression is intellectual, historical or emotional; it does not require manufactured intrigue. A chapter whose only job is to repeat or illustrate an established conclusion should be merged, replaced or removed. Research broadly; narrate selectively. Later evidence should sharpen, complicate or overturn earlier interpretations.

## Craft influences (not compulsory narrative formulas)
- **Folding Ideas:** cumulative causal reasoning, more diagnostic evidence, testing whether a problem is superficial or structural.
- **Defunctland:** question-driven investigations and explanation of systems through real consequences; if the evidence warrants it, reveal a more powerful explanation that makes earlier material look different.
- **NakeyJakey:** natural spoken voice, recognizable game behavior, specific examples and analogies that carry an argument.
- **Summoning Salt:** clear objectives and obstacles before chronological progress, near-misses and breakthroughs.
- **Jon Bois:** the human and historical significance of carefully chosen details, often with quiet conclusions and no tedious recap.

Choose the story engine to match the topic: game design critique, breaking update, chronological development, lore mystery, strange history, positive explanation or personal commentary. Avoid an obligatory contrarian angle.

## Mandatory section design questions
1. What is the motivating question right now?
2. What specific *new* evidence or event is introduced?
3. What does it establish, and how rigorous is the reasoning?
4. How is the viewer's understanding changed?
5. Why does the next passage follow by logic or chronology?

Use these as private tests, not a repeated five-beat script formula. Let evidence correct the initial hypothesis. The central middle should carry the hardest explanation. Don't choose runtime or chapter count before evaluating the material.

## Workflow
**Ideation:** Identify who might watch, why the question matters, what evidence could resolve it and what Cube adds. Verify timely demand when relevant. A direct news story may need no additional spin. Validate the title and thumbnail promise against the research. Kill thin ideas before inflating them.

**Research:** Find dated original sources, exact game versions and status (live vs beta vs announced), developer statements, patch notes, contemporary accounts, implementation details, relevant comparisons, strong objections and counterexamples. Record claim provenance and separate fact, observation, reported opinion, hypothesis and inference. For systems, trace **rules → incentives → choices/behavior → consequences**; a predicted incentive is not automatic evidence that most people acted that way.

**Structure:** Order discoveries by causal or narrative necessity; build reader expectations before results, important constraints before attempted breakthroughs, plausible explanations before warranted reversals. Don't stack sources or topics in arbitrary chapters. Keep only as much context as an informed gaming audience needs.

**Script:** Deliver on the title quickly. Write conversationally, with everyday verbs and game-native language. Prefer recognizable actions and their consequences to abstract commentary. Use concrete examples and analogies that explain more than one thing. A joke or aside should contribute to the experience. Vary cadence naturally; let short decisive lines follow accumulated reasoning. Use “so”, “well”, “anyway”, etc. sparingly but without self-conscious avoidance.

**Review:** Check evidence and causal scope first; then structural development and cuts; then knowledgeable audience assumptions, sentence rhythm, visual feasibility and ending. Does each passage add evidence, orientation, comprehension, character or necessary texture? If none, delete it. End at the supported resolution, without a second conclusion.

## Language
Use **XP, gear, BiS, DPS, raid, loot, GCD, ticks, grind, aggro, skilling, drop rate, boss, buff, nerf, build**, and other relevant terms naturally. Do not explain obvious mechanics to experienced WoW/OSRS viewers. Avoid the singular noun **“player”** for one person (“a player,” “the player,” “each player”); prefer **you, someone, a mage, a raider, a character, an ironman** or rewrite. **“Players”** plural is fine. Avoid authored narration word **“distinction”**.

Cut perfunctory disclaimers, generic hype (“this changes everything”), formulaic rhetorical setups, lists of interchangeable claims, theatrical understatement, repetitive explanations, and excessive **“not X, but Y”** structures. These are frequency and function checks, not inflexible bans on ordinary language. Do not assign the creator an unverified personal experience. The user's approved script edits determine voice preferences where compatible.

## Accuracy, pacing and production
Keep source dates and qualifications when material, especially across WoW eras and OSRS versions. Community anecdotes are evidence of reported experiences, not representative statistics. Respect straightforward, feasible solo-creator visuals: gameplay captures, collectible game assets, licensed stock, simple generated stills, text and basic motion. Publishing results are hypotheses about what worked; compare age, audience, traffic mix and packaging before attributing retention or views to script craft.

## Pre-delivery audit
**What is the question? What are the strongest three or four developments? Where does the evidence get more consequential rather than just more numerous? Which section changes the interpretation of an earlier one? Is each transition necessary? What would the viewer lose if a paragraph vanished? Does it sound like someone fluent in the game talking naturally? Is the ending earned?**
